🔎 Trading Glossary

Plain-language definitions for the core concepts used throughout the guide. Use this page when a setup mentions a term like MSS, OB, FVG, liquidity sweep, or RR.

How to use this glossary: Read the definition first, then follow the related chapter link for examples and checklists. These terms describe educational concepts, not guaranteed market behavior.

Risk-Reward (RR)

The relationship between the amount you can lose if the stop loss is hit and the amount you can gain if the target is reached. A 1:3 RR means risking 1 unit to aim for 3 units.

Related: Risk Management

Risk per Trade

The maximum percentage of account capital you are willing to lose on one trade. In this guide, position size should be adjusted so the total trade risk stays within the 1-2% cap.

Related: Risk Management Basics

Support & Resistance

Support is a price area where buyers may defend the market. Resistance is a price area where sellers may defend it. Treat them as zones, not exact lines.

Related: Support & Resistance

Market Structure

The pattern of swing highs and swing lows. Higher highs and higher lows suggest an uptrend; lower highs and lower lows suggest a downtrend.

Related: Market Structure Check

Liquidity

Areas where many orders may be resting, often around previous highs/lows, equal highs/lows, or obvious support/resistance. In this guide, liquidity is used as a map for where price may react.

Related: Liquidity Grab

Liquidity Sweep / Stop Hunt

A move beyond a visible high or low that triggers orders, then quickly reverses. It is only useful as a setup after confirmation, not as a standalone entry signal.

Related: PO3 Manipulation

Market Structure Shift (MSS)

A change in structure after a sweep or trend leg. For example, after a downtrend, price breaks the most recent lower high with momentum. MSS is used as confirmation that direction may be changing.

Related: Market Structure Shift

Order Block (OB)

The last opposite candle or small zone before a strong impulse move that breaks structure. The guide uses OBs as possible pullback entry zones, especially when they overlap with other confluence.

Related: Institutional Entry

Fair Value Gap (FVG)

A three-candle imbalance where price moved quickly and left an inefficient area between candle 1 and candle 3. Traders often watch for price to revisit that area before continuing.

Related: FVG Entry Zone

Confluence

Multiple reasons supporting the same trade idea, such as higher timeframe trend, key level, volume, candlestick confirmation, OB, and FVG alignment.

Related: Combining Strategies

Power of 3 (PO3)

An intraday model with three phases: accumulation, manipulation, and distribution/expansion. The guide uses PO3 to avoid chasing the first breakout and wait for confirmation.

Related: PO3 Overview

Accumulation

A sideways or quiet phase where price builds a range. In PO3 it often precedes a false breakout. In market cycles it can also describe a longer bottoming phase.

Related: Market Cycle Accumulation

Breakout

A close beyond a support or resistance level. The guide favors breakout trades after confirmation and retest, and warns against chasing the first session breakout during possible PO3 manipulation.

Related: Breakout Strategy

Pullback

A temporary move back toward a key level during a broader trend or after a breakout. Pullbacks can offer cleaner entries than chasing an impulse candle.

Related: Pullback Strategy

Retest

When price returns to a level it recently broke. A successful retest may show that old resistance is acting as support, or old support is acting as resistance.

Related: Breakout Retest

Partial Profit

Closing part of a position before the final target. The guide suggests partials around 1:2 RR, then managing the rest toward the planned target.
